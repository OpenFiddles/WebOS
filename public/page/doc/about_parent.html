About OpenFiddles

“For The Community, By The Community.” OpenFiddles describes itself as a community-minded creative software studio built around openness, contribution, and making technology approachable.

About this page: This overview is based on the OpenFiddles’ Everything Booklet supplied for the WebOS project. It presents the booklet’s own descriptions and historical notes; it is not a corporate registry, independent verification of ownership, or a statement that every project mentioned is currently released or operational.

Who or what is OpenFiddles?

The booklet introduces OpenFiddles as a studio founded by its creator, with a focus on letting people contribute to and modify software and creative projects. It describes a community-oriented approach to making free and open software, and uses “donation-ware” for software that remains free to use while allowing voluntary donations.

Its stated outlook is that progress and sustainable work matter, but people should come first. The founder’s note describes an aspiration for flexible schedules, time to recharge, and giving contributors a voice as the studio develops.

“OpenFiddles is a community, not a corporation. And I’m proud of it.”

— Founder’s note in the booklet

Projects and creative work

WebOS

A browser-first operating environment built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with backend services used for capabilities such as online authentication and storage. The booklet describes WebOS as a project under the OpenFiddles banner.

Qumi / Qumio AI

An AI project described in the booklet as part of OpenFiddles’ work. The booklet includes a separate Qumio AI Terms of Use section; its terms should not be assumed to replace WebOS’s own policies.

Moxira

A game concept mentioned in the founder’s introduction, described as combining inspirations from Minecraft, Terraria, and Vintage Story.

OpenFLAIR

A project introduced in the booklet as a newer technology-focused child project, with ambitions spanning software, hardware, devices, and operating-system variants.

Juniper

The booklet introduces Juniper as the OpenFiddles mascot in an entry dated October 18, 2026, and describes a ringed-planet visual identity.

More projects

The booklet frames OpenFiddles as a place for a range of software, hardware, games, and creative work. Project status and availability can change over time.

How WebOS fits in

In the booklet’s WebOS section, WebOS is described as a browser-first operating environment: its interface is built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with additional backend support. It identifies Vercel as the hosting platform and Supabase for data and authentication, connected through a Node.js backend.

The booklet also notes that many parts can run locally, while online authentication and storage may not be available in a local or guest session. For the current feature set and product details, visit What is WebOS?.

Open-source and licensing

The booklet includes a GNU General Public License version 3 section and says that many OpenFiddles projects use GNU licensing. Licensing is determined by the actual license and notices distributed with each repository, component, or release. A general statement in the booklet does not automatically relicense every project, and GPL-3.0 and AGPL-3.0 are distinct licenses.

For WebOS, consult the repository’s current LICENSE file and the license information page. The WebOS legal documents are available in the UTAC and Acceptable Use Policy.
